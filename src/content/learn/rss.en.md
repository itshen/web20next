## A feed you can subscribe to

RSS is an XML-based format for sharing content. A website publishes entries in a feed; a reader brings feeds from different websites together. In RSS 2.0, RSS stands for Really Simple Syndication. See the [RSS 2.0 specification](https://www.rssboard.org/rss-specification).

## Start with a website you like

1. Open your RSS reader and find its option to add a subscription.
2. Paste in the RSS address provided by the website and save it.
3. Check updates in your reader, then follow links to the original site when something catches your interest.

Some readers can find a feed from a website address. Sites can declare that feed in the page header, as described in the [RSS autodiscovery guide](https://www.rssboard.org/rss-autodiscovery).

## Keep content discoverable

For readers, RSS offers a reading list they choose. For website owners, it gives people a way to keep following their work. You can ask your agent to check whether your site has a feed and help add a subscription link, making new work easier to discover.
